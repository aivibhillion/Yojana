Yojana Chapter 2: A Life-Course Approach to Malnutrition in India

Malnutrition is inter-generational: programmes must follow people from adolescence through pregnancy, infancy and adulthood.

Yojana · Current Affairs Notes · September 2026

Key Points at a Glance

  • Anaemia Mukt Bharat relaunched: new Operational Guidelines (June 2026, 16th Central Council of Health and Family Welfare meeting) — 6x6x6 → 7x7x7, T3 → T4 (adds Track), prophylactic → therapeutic case-based care incl. IV iron.
  • NFHS-5 anaemia: 57% women (15–49) vs 53% in NFHS-4; 67.1% children (6–59 months) vs 58.6%.
  • NFHS-6 fact sheet omitted haemoglobin-based anaemia estimates (capillary sampling inflated prevalence); venous-sample data to come from the ICMR Diet and Biomarkers Survey.
  • First 1,000 days = ~270 days of pregnancy + first 730 days of life. NFHS-6: stunting 35.5% → 29.3%, severe wasting 7.7% → 5.2%, but only 15.3% of children (6–23 months) get an adequate diet.
  • Double burden: adult obesity (15–49) women 24% → 30.7%, men 22.9% → 27.3%; NCDs cause ~63% of all deaths in India.

Anaemia Mukt Bharat: what changed

  • New Operational Guidelines were released in June 2026 at the 16th meeting of the Central Council of Health and Family Welfare.
  • 6x6x6 → 7x7x7: 7th beneficiary group = low-birth-weight babies (0–6 months); 7th intervention = Eating Right; 7th institutional pillar = strengthened monitoring and evaluation.
  • T3 (Test, Treat, Talk) → T4: Track added.
  • Shift from prophylactic distribution to therapeutic, case-based management, including intravenous iron therapy for severe and non-responsive cases.
  • Weekly Iron and Folic Acid Supplementation: distribution often above 80%, but consumption as low as 20% — the gap is in coverage-to-consumption conversion.
  • NFHS-6 fact sheet omitted Hb-based anaemia estimates for the first time in 3+ decades; ICMR Diet and Biomarkers Survey will give venous-sample estimates.
  • Until then, the real-time digital ecosystem — JANANI (Janani Suraksha Yojana + Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram), RBSK and U-WIN portals — is the only active gauge of anaemia, raising the stakes for data integrity.
  • WHO (2019) placed India fifth-highest in anaemia prevalence among women of reproductive age.

First 1,000 days and the stunting–wasting picture

  • First 1,000 days ≈ 270 days of pregnancy + 730 days (2 years) of infancy; brain, immune system and metabolic architecture are laid down; damage is generally irreversible or only partly reversible.
  • NFHS-6: stunting 35.5% → 29.3%; severe wasting 7.7% → 5.2%; underweight 31.8%.
  • Exclusive breastfeeding (<6 months) 95.6%; complementary feeding (6–8 months) 45.9% → 59.5%.
  • Only 15.3% of children aged 6–23 months receive an adequate diet; nearly 1 in 3 under-5 children remain stunted.
  • A child of a mother with no formal schooling is roughly twice as likely to be stunted.
  • ICDS (since 1975) and Anganwadi Centres remain the most extensive platform; gains credited to Anganwadi, POSHAN Abhiyaan, PMMVY, Mission Indradhanush.
  • Author proposes a national protocol linking early stimulation therapy with nutritional rehabilitation; survivors of early stunting/wasting carry higher risk of insulin resistance, obesity and cardio-metabolic disease.

Double burden and institutions

  • NFHS-6 obesity (age 15–49): women 24% → 30.7%; men 22.9% → 27.3%.
  • NCDs account for ~63% of all deaths; nearly a quarter of Indians face premature NCD death before age 70.
  • Over 1.85 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs screen citizens above 30 for hypertension, diabetes and common cancers.
  • NQAS-certified public health facilities: 10 (2016) → 64,671 (May 2026).
  • PM-ABHIM outlay ≈ Rs 64,180 crore; ABDM has issued 70 crore+ health IDs; eSanjeevani 46 crore+ tele-consultations.
  • Demographic dividend window projected to extend to ~2055; next phase needs convergence: Anganwadi–Ayushman Arogya Mandir, ASHA visits–NCD screening register, livelihood missions–nutrition security; Jan Bhagidari/BCC is the key institutional lever.

From disease control to life-course thinking

For four decades public-health discourse was built around vertical, single-disease programmes (smallpox, polio, neonatal tetanus, guinea worm) — effective for infectious threats. The article argues community medicine has moved towards securing the well-being of whole populations, especially vulnerable groups.

Anaemia is the 'mirror to the unfinished agenda': NFHS-5 showed anaemia rising in women (53% → 57%) and children (58.6% → 67.1%), which prompted the Anaemia Mukt Bharat relaunch.

Coverage is not consumption

The micronutrient programmes' weakness, as policymakers argued through the 2010s, lay less in supply chains and more in conversion from coverage to consumption — e.g. WIFS distribution >80% against consumption of ~20%.

The 7x7x7 and T4 changes are described as institutional admissions of this gap; they must be treated as SOPs, not rhetoric. The NFHS-6 data gap on Hb-based estimates raises the stakes for data integrity in the digital ecosystem (JANANI, RBSK, U-WIN).

The double burden

Child undernutrition is easing (stunting 29.3%) even as adult obesity rises sharply; the same household — sometimes the same individual across a lifetime — bears both ends. Nutrition-specific policy cannot offset nutrition-sensitive determinants: income, sanitation, maternal education/health, employment.

Millet promotion is positive but only one component of a family-centred strategy.

Way forward

  • Make 7x7x7/T4 operational: track, treat and follow up cases with reliable data
  • Convergence: Anganwadi ↔ Ayushman Arogya Mandir; ASHA home visits ↔ NCD screen register; rural livelihood missions ↔ nutrition security
  • Link early stimulation therapy with nutritional rehabilitation
  • Behaviour Change Communication and Jan Bhagidari at the centre
