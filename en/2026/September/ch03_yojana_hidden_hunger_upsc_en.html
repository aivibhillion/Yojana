Yojana Chapter 3: Combating Hidden Hunger: The Micronutrient Deficiency Challenge

Enough calories can still hide deficiencies of iron, folate, B12, vitamin A, iodine and zinc.

Yojana · Current Affairs Notes · September 2026

Key Points at a Glance

  • Hidden hunger = chronic deficiency of essential vitamins and minerals, often masked by adequate calorie intake; it hits women, adolescents and children hardest.
  • CNNS 2016–18: vitamin A deficiency 18% (pre-school), 22% (school-age), 16% (adolescents); zinc up to 32%, B12 31%, folate 37% in adolescents; vitamin D deficiency 70–100% of population.
  • Diet gaps: pulses give only 11% of protein (recommended 19%); coarse-grain intake down ~40%; fat 2.5× RDA; salt ~11 g/day (over double WHO limit).
  • SEHAT (ICMR + ICAR): mission-mode programme converging agricultural research with public health via biofortified and traditional crops.
  • If current trends continue, SDG 2 (Zero Hunger) will be missed by 2030; the next frontier is moving from calorie sufficiency to nutritional adequacy.

Concept and causes

  • It is possible to eat enough, feel full and still be deficient in iron, folate, vitamin B12, vitamin A, iodine and zinc.
  • Deficiencies impair growth, immunity, cognitive development and productivity over time.
  • Anaemia is a major manifestation; iron deficiency is the chief contributor but anaemia can also reflect other deficiencies and poor absorption/utilisation.
  • Addressing it needs more than supplements: dietary quality, diversity and bioavailability.

Data from CNNS 2016–18 and diet surveys

  • Vitamin A deficiency: 18% pre-school, 22% school-age, 16% adolescents.
  • Adolescents: zinc up to 32%, vitamin B12 31%, folate 37%; vitamin D deficiency spans 70–100% of the general population.
  • Nearly half of protein intake comes from cereals; pulses supply only 11% (recommended 19%).
  • Coarse-grain consumption down ~40%; fat intake 2.5× RDA; 42% of households miss 90% of fibre needs; salt 11 g/day.

India's response and global lessons

  • Targeted channels distribute vitamin A and iron-folic acid (IFA) supplements; fortification standards cover rice, wheat flour, milk, edible oil and double-fortified salt.
  • Safety nets as delivery platforms: PM POSHAN, Mission POSHAN 2.0, PDS (Take-Home Rations and Hot Cooked Meals).
  • Biofortification: high-iron pearl millet improved iron status and cognitive outcomes among adolescents; homestead gardening and small indigenous fish helped in South/Southeast Asia.
  • Junk-food regulation: curb HFSS marketing to children, Front-of-Pack Labelling, health taxes on sugar-sweetened beverages; Chile is the cited precedent.
  • BCC on bioavailability: germination, soaking, fermentation, and combining iron sources with vitamin C.
  • Vision statement: 'Kuposhan Mukt Bharat'; the six policy directions are inter-ministerial convergence, diversified PDS/THR, scaling biofortification and fortification, curbing junk food, bioavailability education, and decentralised T4 tracking.

Why 'enough food' is not 'enough nutrition'

India has made progress on food insecurity and calorie deficiency, but diet quality lags: cereals dominate protein intake while pulses supply 11% against a recommended 19%, coarse grains have declined ~40%, and fat and salt are far above recommended levels. The result is the coexistence of micronutrient deficiency with overweight and diet-related NCDs.

South Asia's improvement has been too slow; the article warns SDG 2 will be missed by 2030 on current trends.

Delivery platforms and their limits

Supplementation and fortification are necessary but insufficient without absorption and utilisation; hence the emphasis on bioavailability (germination, soaking, fermentation, iron with vitamin C).

Social-protection platforms (PM POSHAN, Mission POSHAN 2.0, PDS) offer leverage to shift from calorie-dense to nutrient-dense provisioning — by adding millets (ragi, bajra), pulses, oilseeds and fortified commodities to PDS baskets and Anganwadi THR.

Agriculture–health convergence

Procurement and production incentives should favour nutrient-dense crops, not just staple grains. SEHAT (ICMR + ICAR) is the institutional vehicle: biofortified crops (high-iron pearl millet, zinc-dense wheat), traditional crops and nutrition-sensitive agriculture.

Way forward

  • Inter-ministerial convergence (WCD, Health, Agriculture, Consumer Affairs/Food & PD)
  • Diversify PDS/THR; scale biofortification with quality assurance and supply-chain tracking
  • Regulate HFSS marketing, FoPL, health taxes; remove empty-calorie items near schools/Anganwadis
  • Expand T4 with digital linkage so deficiencies get continuous case management
