Yojana Chapter 11: Biofloc Technology: A New Green Revolution in Nutritional Security and Sustainable Aquaculture

A zero-waste, high-density system that turns fish waste into protein — saving water and land while making fish protein affordable.

Yojana · Current Affairs Notes · September 2026

Key Points at a Glance

  • Biofloc = microscopic aggregates of bacteria, fungi, protozoa, algae and particles formed when an external carbon source is added (C:N ratio 12:1 to 15:1) so heterotrophic bacteria absorb toxic ammonia and convert it to cellular protein.
  • In conventional farming only 20–30% of feed becomes fish biomass; ~70% is released as nitrogenous waste (toxic ammonia and nitrite). Floc has 30–45% crude protein (dry weight) and acts as natural supplementary feed.
  • Efficiency: up to 90% water saving (conventional ponds need 30,000–50,000 L per kg fish); tanks of just 40–50 m²; 50–150 fish/m³ vs 1–3 in ordinary ponds; 20–30% less artificial feed.
  • Key parameters: dissolved oxygen ≥ 5 mg/L; pH 7.2–7.8; alkalinity 100–150 mg/L; floc volume 15–30 mL/L (Imhoff cone). Power failure of 30–40 minutes can drop oxygen to zero.
  • PMMSY subsidy: 40% for the general category and up to 60% for women, SC and ST beneficiaries; contributes to SDGs 2, 3, 6, 12 and 14.

How biofloc works

  • India is the world's second-largest fish-producing country; fisheries support food security, employment, rural incomes and exports.
  • Principle: convert harmful fish excreta into high-quality organic protein through biochemical cycles — turning waste into nutrition.
  • Add a soluble carbon source (molasses, rice bran or tapioca powder) and keep C:N at 12:1–15:1; heterotrophic bacteria absorb dissolved ammonia and convert it to cellular protein.
  • Why toxins matter: ammonia damages fish gills; nitrite binds haemoglobin to form methaemoglobin (brown blood disease), cutting oxygen transport.
  • Biofloc contains microorganisms, natural probiotics, minerals and protein (30–45% crude protein dry weight); it suppresses harmful bacteria such as Vibrio, reducing disease risk and antibiotic need.
  • In conventional farming only 20–30% of commercial feed is converted to fish body mass; ~70% enters water as nitrogenous waste (uneaten food, faeces, gills) accumulating mainly as toxic ammonia and nitrite — the problem biofloc addresses.

Resource efficiency and management

  • Water: conventional ponds need 30,000–50,000 litres per kg of fish; biofloc can save up to 90% (zero-liquid discharge; only evaporation/cleaning losses are replenished).
  • Land and density: tarpaulin or concrete tanks of 40–50 m²; 50–150 fish per m³ vs 1–3 in ordinary ponds.
  • Oxygen: continuous aeration; keep dissolved oxygen above 5 mg/L using root blowers, ring blowers or silicone-tube diffusers; aeration also keeps floc suspended.
  • Water chemistry: pH 7.2–7.8; total alkalinity 100–150 mg/L; daily floc check with an Imhoff cone — ideal 15–30 mL/L; remove excess sludge.
  • Species: GIFT tilapia (omnivorous, eats floc), pangasius, singhi, magur, pabda, and white shrimp (coastal/saline, export-oriented).
  • Risks: power interruption is the biggest threat (use generator backup, inverter, dual blowers); excessive sunlight, heavy rain or C:N imbalance can cause a floc crash and sudden ammonia spike; training from ICAR, CIFA or other authentic institutions is advised before entering.

Livelihoods, policy and future

  • Small farmers, women's SHGs and youth can run units with 4–6 tanks on rooftops, backyards or small plots — low land and capital barrier.
  • Feed can be up to 70% of fish-farming operating cost; biofloc cuts artificial feed use by 20–30%, raising net income.
  • PMMSY (Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana): 40% subsidy (general) and up to 60% (women, SC, ST) for biofloc units, tanks, aeration and power backup.
  • Zero liquid discharge prevents polluted water entering natural sources; links to SDG 6 (clean water), 2 (zero hunger), 3 (health), 12 (responsible consumption) and 14 (life below water).
  • Future: AI, IoT, solar-powered aeration and smart sensors that track pH, oxygen and ammonia and alert farmers' phones.
  • Nutritional angle: antibiotic-free, chemical-free fish rich in omega-3 fatty acids and digestible protein can help address anaemia and protein deficiency among children and women — linking aquaculture to the nutrition mission.

The problem biofloc solves

Climate change, declining freshwater, shrinking land and environmental waste from traditional aquaculture have created a crisis in the sector. In ponds, only 20–30% of feed turns into fish; the rest becomes nitrogenous waste that accumulates as toxic ammonia and nitrite — damaging gills and causing brown blood disease.

Biofloc turns this waste into a resource: heterotrophic bacteria, fed an external carbon source at a C:N ratio of 12:1–15:1, absorb ammonia and build microbial protein that fish eat continuously.

Economics, equity and nutrition

Feed is up to 70% of operating cost; biofloc reduces artificial feed by 20–30%. Because tanks need only 40–50 m² and a few units can sit on rooftops or backyards, small farmers, women's SHGs and unemployed youth — traditionally excluded for want of land and capital — can participate. PMMSY subsidies (40%; up to 60% for women/SC/ST) lower entry costs.

Nutritionally, antibiotic-free, omega-3-rich fish offers affordable protein that could help address anaemia and protein deficiency among children and women.

Risks and limits

Biofloc is a highly sensitive technical system: electricity interruption is the greatest threat (oxygen can reach zero within 30–40 minutes at high density); floc crashes, sunlight, rain and C:N imbalance cause ammonia spikes; daily monitoring (pH, DO, alkalinity, ammonia, nitrite, floc volume) is essential. Entry without training is risky — ICAR, CIFA or similar institutions are recommended.

Way forward

  • Training and extension through ICAR/CIFA and other authentic institutions
  • Reliable power backup: generator, inverter, dual blowers; solar aeration
  • Sensor/IoT/AI-based monitoring for water chemistry
  • Use PMMSY support to include women, SC/ST farmers and youth
