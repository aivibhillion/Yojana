Yojana Chapter 1: Maximising India's Demographic Dividend: Investing in the Early Years

A large young population becomes an economic advantage only when children grow up healthy, well-nourished and ready to learn.

Yojana · Current Affairs Notes · September 2026

Key Points at a Glance

  • NFHS-6 (2023–24): institutional deliveries 88.6% → 90.6%; full immunisation (12–23 months) 83.8% → 87.1%; under-5 stunting 35.5% → 29.3%.
  • Navchetana = framework for early stimulation (birth–3 years); Aadharshila = ECCE curriculum (3–6 years); both by the Ministry of Women and Child Development.
  • Health, nutrition, responsive caregiving, protection, stimulation and early learning must be seen as one continuum.
  • ASER 2024: rural Grade 3 children who can read a Grade 2 text rose from 16.3% (2022) to 23.4% (2024).
  • EAC-PM estimate: effective foundational-learning investment could yield gains equal to 7.39% of GDP over 20 years (a modelled estimate, not a forecast).

Early childhood: why it matters

  • Early childhood development (ECD) is an investment in India's human capital, not just a social-sector priority.
  • The first six years see rapid cognitive, language, physical and socio-emotional development.
  • Navchetana: National Framework for Early Childhood Stimulation (birth–3 yrs). Aadharshila: National Curriculum for Early Childhood Care and Education (3–6 yrs).
  • Serve-and-return interactions (talking, responding, singing, playing) in the 0–3 age group build language, emotional security and self-regulation.
  • Home-based models: Maharashtra's Aarambh, Odisha's Aama Kuni Pila.
  • Crèche models: Haryana State Creche Policy-2022; Karnataka's Koosina Mane.
  • Meghalaya's Early Childhood Development Mission brings health, nutrition, early learning and caregiving together.
  • For ages 3–6, learning should be play-based, age-appropriate and in the child's own language — not formal schooling brought down to younger ages.
  • Technology (including AI-based screening) is an enabler, not a substitute for caregivers; human oversight, safeguards and referral mechanisms are essential.
  • Developmental assessment is meaningful only when it leads to guidance, referral or intervention — timely support, not just data generation.
  • Community platforms: ECCE Days, Village Health, Sanitation and Nutrition Committees (VHSNCs), SHGs, Panchayati Raj Institutions, Mothers' Groups, youth groups.

Data points

  • UDISE+ 2024–25: 24.69 crore students enrolled in school education.
  • NFHS-6: children breastfed within one hour of birth rose from 41.8% to 50.1%.
  • PARAKH Rashtriya Sarvekshan 2024 assessed 21 lakh+ students of Grades 3, 6 and 9 across all 36 States/UTs.
  • NAS 2021 scores are not a direct measure of learning loss in the same cohort — they are cross-sectional grade averages.

Why the dividend is conditional

A large young population is only a potential advantage. It converts into an economic advantage when children grow up healthy, well-nourished, confident and ready to learn — and those foundations are laid before school entry.

Early gaps in nutrition, stimulation, responsive care and learning compound: they shape school progression, skills and ultimately productivity. That is why the article frames ECD as human-capital investment.

The economic case: the EAC-PM report on foundational literacy and numeracy estimates gains equivalent to 7.39% of GDP over 20 years under its modelling assumptions — an answer should present this as a modelled estimate, not a forecast.

Continuum, convergence and the role of States

Policy is moving towards a continuum: Navchetana (0–3) and Aadharshila (3–6) give the Anganwadi system a stimulation and learning backbone alongside nutrition and health services.

The article names three priorities for States/UTs:

  • bringing departments together around the child (Meghalaya's ECD Mission as an example)
  • evaluating promising interventions rigorously (Aarambh, Aama Kuni Pila, crèche models)
  • taking evidence-based approaches to scale

Critical appraisal

NFHS-6 shows real gains (stunting down ~6 percentage points), but learning data (ASER, NAS, PARAKH) show foundational gaps persist — even the improved ASER figure means roughly three in four rural Grade 3 children still cannot read a Grade 2 text.

Caution on data: NAS grade-wise averages (57→32) come from different cohorts and should not be read as learning loss; digital/AI screening is useful only if it triggers referral and support.

Crèche access matters especially where mothers work — quality, standards and availability are the gaps the State models try to address.

Way forward

  • Strengthen the Anganwadi Worker's role (training, materials, parent engagement) in play-based early learning
  • Expand home-visit and caregiver-meeting models for the 0–3 group
  • Scale quality crèches with clear standards
  • Use technology as an enabler with human oversight and referral pathways
  • Build rigorous evaluation into State pilots before scaling

Mains Practice

India's demographic dividend will depend less on how many young people it has and more on what capabilities they acquire. Discuss with reference to early childhood development frameworks. (15 marks · 250 words)

  • State innovations: Aarambh, Aama Kuni Pila, Meghalaya ECD Mission
  • Economic rationale: EAC-PM 7.39% of GDP (modelled)
