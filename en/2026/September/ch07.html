Yojana Chapter 7: The Transformational Journey of Swachh Bharat Mission (Grameen)

From toilets to ODF Plus: how a national programme became a Jan Andolan for rural sanitation and public health.

Yojana · Current Affairs Notes · September 2026

Key Points at a Glance

  • SBM(G) launched 2 October 2014; 2014–19: 10 crore+ household toilets (IHHLs) and 67,000+ Community Sanitary Complexes; rural coverage ~39% → 100%; all districts ODF on 2 Oct 2019.
  • SBM(G) 2.0 (launched 2020): ODF sustainability, solid and liquid waste management, visual cleanliness — villages move from ODF → ODF Plus (Model); extended till FY 2026-27 with the same budget.
  • Cumulative since 2014: 12.17 crore+ household toilets; 2.78 lakh+ CSCs; as of 24 July 2026, 5.18 lakh+ villages are ODF Plus (Model).
  • Impact: WHO — ~3 lakh diarrhoeal deaths averted (2014–19); UNICEF — ODF households save close to Rs 50,000/year; *Nature* — tens of thousands of child deaths prevented annually.
  • Governance: sanitation is a State subject → decentralised design; GPDP planning, eGramSwaraj, geo-tagging, PFMS, SNA-SPARSH; convergence with 15th/16th Finance Commission grants and VB-G RAM G.

Phase 1: SBM(G), 2014–2019

  • Launched on 2 October 2014, following the Prime Minister's 15 August 2014 address on the dignity and health of rural women.
  • 2014–2019: 10 crore+ Individual Household Latrines (IHHLs) and 67,000+ Community Sanitary Complexes (CSCs).
  • Rural sanitation coverage ~39% (2014) → 100% (2019); all districts declared Open Defecation Free (ODF) by 2 October 2019.
  • Safer sanitation broke the faecal–oral transmission cycle linked to malnutrition and poor health outcomes.
  • Community approaches, behaviour change, mobilisation and collective ownership (Jan Bhagidari) were the cornerstone — described as possibly among the world's largest behaviour-change movements.

Phase 2: SBM(G) 2.0, 2020 onwards

  • Launched in 2020: focus on ODF sustainability, solid and liquid waste management and visual cleanliness — ODF → ODF Plus (Model).
  • Extended till FY 2026-27 with the same budgetary allocation.
  • Cumulative: 12.17 crore+ household toilets and 2.78 lakh+ CSCs since 2014; 5.18 lakh+ villages ODF Plus (Model) as of 24 July 2026.
  • Components: scientific waste management (household and community), greywater, plastic waste, faecal sludge management, GOBARdhan (Galvanizing Organic Bio-Agro Resources) and visual cleanliness.
  • Sanitation is a State subject: SBM(G) 2.0 is a decentralised framework letting States/UTs choose solutions within scheme design and funding norms.
  • Illustrated by a cluster-based rural FSTP (faecal sludge treatment plant) in Indore district, Madhya Pradesh, and a Gobardhan plant in Jammu district, J&K.

Governance, monitoring and impact

  • Planning is integrated into the Gram Panchayat Development Plan (GPDP) with Gram Panchayat and Village Water & Sanitation Committee; eGramSwaraj supports planning and monitoring.
  • Transparency tools: mandatory geo-tagging of assets, mobile-based verification, PFMS, SNA-SPARSH, digital monitoring and multi-level verification.
  • Financial convergence with 15th/16th Finance Commission grants and Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Grameen) (VB-G RAM G).
  • National campaigns: Swachhata Hi Seva and Swachhata Pakhwada; sanitation has also featured in Mann Ki Baat.
  • Impact: WHO — nearly 3 lakh diarrhoeal deaths averted (2014–19); UNICEF — ODF households save close to Rs 50,000 a year and face less groundwater contamination.
  • Next behaviour goal: Gram Panchayats and communities taking ownership and Operation & Maintenance (O&M) of community assets so that they stay functional, self-sustaining and financially viable.

From infrastructure to behaviour to sustainability

SBM(G) 1.0 built infrastructure at scale (10 crore+ toilets in five years) and, more importantly, shifted deeply entrenched behaviour and social norms — a transition from government programme to Jan Andolan.

SBM(G) 2.0 moves from toilets to systems: solid and liquid waste management, greywater, plastic and faecal sludge management, GOBARdhan, visual cleanliness — an ODF-to-ODF-Plus trajectory. These components are treated as interconnected, not separate interventions.

Governance design: decentralised, digital, convergent

Because sanitation is a State subject, the scheme offers flexibility on solutions while holding to design and funding norms. Gram Panchayats plan via GPDP and eGramSwaraj, with finance converging from Finance Commission grants and VB-G RAM G.

Transparency is built in through geo-tagging, mobile verification, PFMS and SNA-SPARSH.

Health and economic returns

The article cites WHO (≈3 lakh diarrhoeal deaths averted, 2014–19), UNICEF (≈Rs 50,000 annual savings per ODF household; lower groundwater contamination) and *Nature* (tens of thousands of child deaths prevented annually). Sanitation is thus framed as a public-health and nutrition intervention, linking to stunting through faecal–oral transmission.

Challenges and way forward

  • Sustaining behaviour and usage once ODF status is achieved (ODF sustainability)
  • Community ownership of O&M so assets stay functional and financially viable
  • Capacity of Gram Panchayats for waste management and FSM across diverse States
  • Keeping data credible: geo-tagging and multi-level verification
